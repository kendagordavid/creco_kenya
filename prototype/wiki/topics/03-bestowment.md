# Bestowment of Public Benefit Organization Status

## How Existing Organizations Can Acquire Public Benefit Organization Status under the Public Benefits Organizations Act, 2013 and the PBO Regulations, 2026

## Why this Summary Matters

Kenya's nonprofit sector is diverse, with many organizations pursuing public benefit objectives. Some nonprofit organizations (NPOs) are legally incorporated under the Public Benefit Organizations (PBO) Act, while others are registered under laws such as the **Companies Act**, the **Trustees (Perpetual Succession) Act**, **the Societies Act and the Community Groups Registration Act**. Although these organizations already have legal personality, they may wish to receive the benefits, and recognition offered under the Public Benefits Organizations Act, 2013.

To facilitate this, the Act introduces the concept of “**bestowment of Public Benefit Organization status”**. This approach allows an existing organization to retain its original legal identity while being formally recognized as a Public Benefit Organization, without the need to dissolve and re-register under the PBO Act.

The Act provides the legal basis for this bestowment process, while the Public Benefits Organizations Regulations, 2026 prescribe the application procedure, documentation, timelines and forms.

## What is Bestowment of Public Benefit Organization Status?

Bestowment of PBO Status is the legal process through which the Authority formally grants public benefit status to nonprofit organizations incorporated under other laws, such as the Companies Act. This means that NPOs that are not registered as PBOs may still be recognized by the Authority as public benefit organizations.

## Distinction between Registration and Bestowment

Unlike registration under Part II of the Act, bestowment does **not** create a new legal entity. Instead, it grants an organization that is carrying out public benefit activities, the status of Public Benefit Organization, even though it is registered under another law.

In simple terms:

- **Registration** creates a new legal entity – Public Benefit Organization -] under the Act.
- **Bestowment** grants PBO status to an organization that already exists under another law.

This distinction is important because the legal identity of the organization remains unchanged after bestowment.

## Why Seek Bestowment of Public Benefit Status?

An organization may seek bestowment for several reasons.

- Legal recognition: It allows the organization to acquire legal recognition as no organization may claim to be a Public Benefit Organization or receive the benefits of PBO status unless it is registered under the Act or has been granted PBO status by the Authority.
- Access to benefits: It enables the organization to access the rights, privileges and benefits available to PBOs under the Act, including eligibility for incentives and recognition under the PBO legal framework, subject to applicable laws.
- Public confidence: Bestowment demonstrates that the organization has met the standards of governance, accountability and public benefit required under the Act, thereby enhancing public confidence among donors, partners, beneficiaries and government institutions.

## Which Organizations May Apply for Bestowment?

Section 7 of the Act provides that an organization incorporated under another written law may apply to the Authority for the **bestowment of Public Benefit Organization status**, provided it satisfies the requirements prescribed under the Act.

The Regulations further clarify that an eligible organization must be:

- incorporated under a relevant Kenyan law;
- be in good standing with its current registering body;
- not fall within the excluded categories under section 5(2) of the Act,
- have been operating in or from Kenya and undertaking a public benefit activity for at least three years;
- and have a governing body of at least three persons or directors, one-third of whom must be Kenyan nationals residing in Kenya.

Eligible organizations may include:

- companies limited by guarantee;
- trusts incorporated under the Trustees (Perpetual Succession) Act; and
- other non-profit legal entities established under Kenyan law whose primary purpose is to undertake public benefit activities and which are not excluded under section 5(2) of the Act.

The organization must be incorporated under a relevant Kenyan law and must also show that its purposes, activities, governance and track record satisfy the Act and the Regulations.

## Application Procedure

The Regulations prescribe the procedure for applying for bestowment.

An application must be submitted to the Public Benefits Organizations Regulatory Authority using the prescribed form in the Second Schedule and accompanied by the prescribed fee.

The following documents must accompany the application:

- the certificate of incorporation or registration under the relevant law;
- the governing instrument, such as its constitution, trust deed or memorandum and articles of association;
- minutes of the meeting containing the resolution to seek PBO status

details of the members of its governing body or officials;

- the entity’s registered and physical addresses; and

Three years of audited accounts These documents enable the Authority to determine whether the organization satisfies the legal requirements for bestowment.

## Conditions for Bestowment

The Act requires the Authority to be satisfied that the applicant genuinely qualifies as a Public Benefit Organization. Although an organization may already be incorporated under another law, it is **not automatically entitled** to PBO status.

The Authority must still apply the **public benefit test** contained in Regulation 6.

Accordingly, the Authority considers whether:

- the organization's activities fall within recognised public benefit areas;
- the activities directly provide a genuine benefit to the public or a section of the public;
- beneficiaries can be clearly identified; and
- the organization does not improperly benefit directors, their immediate family members or close business associates.

This ensures that only organizations genuinely serving the public interest receive PBO status.

## Assessment by the Authority

Upon receiving a complete application, the Authority examines whether the applicant meets the requirements of both the Act and the Regulations. In particular, the Authority considers:

- the organization's legal status: whether it is established as a nonprofit organization;
- its governing documents;
- whether its objectives are exclusively or primarily public benefit objectives;
- whether its governance arrangements promote accountability;
- whether it satisfies the public benefit test; and
- whether all prescribed documentation has been submitted

If additional information is required, the Authority may request clarification or supporting documentation before making a decision.

## Grant of Bestowment

Where the Authority is satisfied that all legal requirements have been met, it grants the organization **Public Benefit Organization status** and issues the prescribed certificate.

The certificate serves as official confirmation that the organization has received public benefit organization status under the PBO Act while retaining its original legal incorporation.

Unlike organizations registered directly under the Act, the organization continues to derive its legal personality from the law under which it was originally incorporated.

## Legal Effect of Bestowment

Bestowment has important legal consequences.

The organization:

- retains its existing legal identity;
- continues to be governed by its incorporating statute except where the PBO Act imposes additional obligations;
- becomes subject to the oversight framework established under the PBO Act;
- acquires the rights and benefits available to Public Benefit Organizations under the Act; and
- assumes the reporting and accountability obligations applicable to PBOs.

## Can Bestowment Be Refused?

Yes. The Authority may refuse an application where the organization fails to satisfy the requirements of the Act or the Regulations.

Examples include where:

- the organization's purposes are not public benefit purposes;
- its governing documents permit distribution of profits to members;
- its activities primarily benefit private persons;
- governance arrangements are inconsistent with the Act; or
- the prescribed documents have not been submitted.

When the application is refused, the principles of fair administrative action require that the applicants are informed of the reasons for the decision. and afforded the remedies available under the Act.

## Responsibilities after Bestowment

Receiving PBO status is not the end of the compliance process. An organization granted bestowment must comply with the obligations imposed under the Act and the Regulations, including:

- maintaining appropriate governance structures;
- submitting annual reports and financial statements where required;
- notifying the Authority of material changes;
- complying with lawful directions issued under the Act; and
- continuing to operate for public benefit purposes.

Failure to comply may result in regulatory action, including suspension or cancellation of PBO status, as discussed in **Summary 7**.

## Common Misconceptions: Is it true that...

## "Every company limited by guarantee automatically becomes a PBO?"

No. A company limited by guarantee that would like to obtain PBO Status must first apply for bestowment and satisfy the requirements of the Act and the Regulations before acquiring PBO status.

## "An organization loses its legal identity after Bestowment?"

No. The organization retains its original legal status under the relevant law. Bestowment confers recognition of its status as a Public Benefit Organization.

## "Bestowment exempts an organization from complying with its original law?"

No. The organization must continue to comply with the law under which it was incorporated as well as the applicable provisions of the PBO Act.

## Practical Tips

Before applying for bestowment:

- Review your governing documents to ensure they reflect public benefit purposes.
- Confirm that your activities meet the requirements under the public benefit test.
- Ensure your governance arrangements comply with the standards expected under the Act.
- Prepare all necessary incorporation documents and supporting records.
- Verify that your constitution or trust deed contains a non-distribution clause.
- Ensure your records show that you are engaged in ongoing public benefit activities.

## Key Takeaways

- Bestowment allows an organization that is already incorporated under another law the chance to acquire Public Benefit Organization status without having to re-register.
- The organization retains its original legal identity after bestowment.
- The Authority must be satisfied that the organization genuinely serves public benefit purposes.
- The public benefit test under the Regulations applies equally to applications for registration as well as for bestowment.
- Organizations granted bestowment enjoy the rights and benefits of PBOs while acquiring the governance, reporting and accountability obligations applicable under the Act.
