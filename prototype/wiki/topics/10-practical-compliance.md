# Practical Guide to Compliance

## Key Forms, Timelines, Fees and Material Changes under the Public Benefits Organizations Regulations, 2026

## Why this Summary Matters

The obligation to comply with the Public Benefits Organizations Act, 2013 does not end once an organization is registered. Public Benefit Organizations have ongoing legal obligations that help ensure transparency, accountability and effective regulation throughout their lifecycle.

The Public Benefits Organizations Regulations, 2026 operationalise the Act by prescribing the forms to be used, the procedures for making applications, the timelines for submitting reports, the fees payable for various services and the notifications that organizations must provide when significant changes occur.

This summary brings together these practical requirements into a single compliance guide that organizations can use as a quick reference. It complements the earlier summaries by focusing on what organizations need to do, when they need to do it, and how they should comply.

## Why Compliance Matters

Compliance is not simply about avoiding penalties. It helps organizations maintain their legal status, preserve public confidence, strengthen donor credibility, demonstrate accountability, avoid regulatory disputes and access opportunities available to registered PBOs.

Good compliance also supports effective organizational management and long-term sustainability.

## Prescribed Forms under the Regulations

The Regulations prescribe standard forms that must be used when dealing with the Public Benefits Organizations Regulatory Authority. These forms promote consistency, completeness and administrative efficiency.

The key prescribed forms include:

- **Form 1:** Application for registration as a PBO or international PBO.
- **Form 2:** Certificate of registration as a PBO.
- **Form 3:** Application for bestowment of PBO status.
- **Form 4:** Certificate of bestowment of PBO status.
- **Form 5:** Certificate of registration as an international PBO.
- **Form 6:** Application for exemption from registration as an international PBO.
- **Form 7:** Permit to operate as an exempt international PBO.
- **Forms 8 and 9:** Notices relating to intended cancellation, suspension, cancellation or suspension.
- **Form 10:** Application for restoration or reinstatement.
- **Forms 11 and 12:** Application and certificate for recognition of a forum or federation of forums.
- **Form 13:** Annual report.
- **Form 14:** Notification of material changes.
- **Forms 15 and 16:** Notification and certificate of change of name.

Organizations should always ensure that they use the latest prescribed forms issued by the Authority.

**Important Transition Note:** Form 1 is used for fresh registration applications. It may also be used by organizations transitioning from the NGO framework into the PBO framework, to provide updated information to the Authority for purposes of compliance. This transition process should be distinguished from the registration of a completely new organization. A recent High Court decision affirmed that NGOs previously registered under the repealed NGO Co-ordination Act will automatically transition into the PBO framework without losing their legal identity.

**Important Note on Forums and Federations:** Membership of Self-Regulation Forums and Federations under the Regulations is not compulsory. A Public Benefit Organization may choose whether or not to join a forum or federation. Non-membership should not, by itself, affect a PBO’s registration status, rights or compliance standing. A recent High Court decision confirmed that compulsory federation membership is inconsistent with the freedom of association. The provisions of the PBO Act on membership of the National Federation of PBOs should be interpreted in line with this decision.

## Supporting Documents

Most applications submitted under the Regulations must be accompanied by supporting documentation. Depending on the application, these documents may include:

- the constitution or other governing instrument;
- certificate of incorporation or registration;
- proof of name reservation;
- minutes or resolutions authorising the application or change;
- particulars of directors, officials or governing body members;
- information on physical and postal address;
- proof of good standing where required;
- proof of source of funding for international organizations where required;
- audited accounts where required; and
- other documents specified in the Regulations.

Incomplete applications may delay processing or result in requests for additional information.

## Statutory Timelines

The Regulations establish timelines for different compliance obligations. Organizations should pay close attention to deadlines relating to name reservation, applications, annual reporting, notification of material changes, restoration, reinstatement and responses to notices from the Authority.

For example, name reservation is valid for 60 days and may be extended for a further period not exceeding 60 days. Registration, bestowment, international registration and exemption applications are generally determined within 60 days. Restoration and reinstatement applications must be determined within 14 days. Annual reports are submitted within the timeline set by section 31 of the Act, which is within six months after the end of the financial year.

Meeting statutory deadlines helps organizations avoid unnecessary regulatory action. The following table outlines the key requirements and timelines for various legal processes.

## Annual Compliance Calendar

It is recommended that every registered PBO should develop an annual compliance calendar. This calendar should track:

- scheduled meetings of the governing body meetings;
- preparation of annual financial statements;
- completion of audits when required;
- submission of the annual report using Form 13;
- review of governance documents;
- review of internal policies;
- updates to records and asset inventories; and
- statutory notifications where changes occur.

A compliance calendar helps ensure that important obligations are duly observed and not overlooked.

## Material Changes Requiring Notification

One of the most important obligations under the Regulations is to notify the Authority whenever significant changes occur within the organization.

For PBOs, Regulation 29 identifies the following as material changes requiring notification:

- Changes to the constitution or other constitutive document;
- Changes in the composition of the governing body;
- Changes in the particulars of directors or officials provided during registration;
- Changes to the physical or postal address of the principal place of operation;
- Changes to banking arrangements; and
- Changes regarding the authorised agent, in the case of an international PBO.

Depending on the circumstances, other practical changes may also need to be considered carefully because they may affect the information held by the Authority or the organization’s legal status.

Changes to the membership of the governing body must be notified within 30 days. Changes to particulars provided during registration must be notified within 60 days. Other material changes generally need to be reported within 60 days. Where a material change occurs in an exempt international PBO or an organization conferred with PBO status, the organization must first seek prior approval from its principal regulator and then notify the Authority within 30 days of receiving that approval.

**Governance Compliance Reminder:** When notifying the Authority of changes to the governing body, organizations should continue to comply with the governance requirements contained in the Act and Regulations. A registered PBO should maintain a governing body consisting of at least five persons, with at least three of them not being related to one another. Additionally, the organization should continue to satisfy any applicable nationality and residency requirements prescribed by law.

## Change of Name

A change of name is treated separately under Regulation 32. A PBO may change its name by a passing a resolution in accordance with its constitution, by direction of the Authority, or by order of the Court.

Where the organization initiates the change, it must submit a notice of change of name using Form 15. This notice should be accompanied by the required supporting documents, including minutes of the meeting where the resolution was passed, proof of reservation of the proposed name, and the prescribed fee. If the change is approved, the Authority will issue a certificate of change of name using Form 16.

## Maintaining Accurate Records

Compliance depends on maintaining complete and accurate organizational records. Every PBO should maintain:

- its constitution or governing instrument;
- registration certificate or certificate of bestowment;
- governing body records;
- minutes of meetings;
- financial and accounting records;
- bank records;
- asset inventory;
- grant agreements and contracts;
- employment records;
- statutory correspondence; and
- reports submitted to the Authority.

Well-maintained records simplify reporting, audits and regulatory inquiries.

## Internal Compliance Systems

The following are recommended good practices that help organizations establish internal procedures for monitoring compliance:

- assigning responsibility for statutory reporting;
- maintaining a compliance calendar;
- conducting periodic governance reviews;
- reviewing reporting deadlines;
- retaining copies of all submissions made to the Authority;
- keeping electronic and physical records securely; and
- briefing board members and staff on their compliance responsibilities.

Organizations with strong internal compliance systems are less likely to experience regulatory difficulties.

## Electronic Submission and Communication

Regulation 41 allows organizations to submit documents required under the Regulations electronically. Where the Authority provides electronic filing or communication systems, organizations should maintain active email addresses, regularly monitor official communications, keep login credentials secure, update contact information promptly and retain proof of electronic submissions.

Electronic communication can improve efficiency, but it does not remove the organization’s responsibility to comply with legal requirements.

## Consequences of Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with the Regulations may result in requests for additional information, directions from the Authority, delayed processing of applications, compliance inquiries, suspension, cancellation, deregistration or penalties in serious cases.

Most compliance issues can be avoided through timely reporting, accurate record-keeping and prompt communication with the Authority.

## Practical Compliance Checklist

Every registered Public Benefit Organization should ensure that it complies with the PBO Act and Regulations. It can keep track of its compliance through:

- Keeping its constitution and governance documents up to date.
- Holding regular governing body meetings and record minutes.
- Maintaining accurate financial and accounting records.
- Submitting annual reports on time.
- Notifying the Authority promptly of material changes.
- Keeping copies of all correspondence with the Authority.
- Reviewing legal compliance at least once each year.
- Training staff and governing body members on compliance responsibilities.
- Maintaining proper document retention systems.
- Seeking clarification from the Authority whenever legal requirements are uncertain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## Do I need to notify the Authority every time a board member changes?

Yes. Changes in the composition of the governing body are material changes and should be reported using the prescribed procedures under Regulation 29.

## Can I amend my constitution without informing the Authority?

No. Amendments to a PBO’s constitution or governing instrument are material changes and should be reported to the Authority in accordance with the Regulations.

## What happens if I miss a reporting deadline?

The Authority may require the organization to comply, request additional information or take regulatory action depending on the circumstances. Organizations should communicate with the Authority promptly if they anticipate delays.

## Should I keep copies of documents submitted to the Authority?

Yes. Organizations should retain copies of all applications, reports, financial statements and correspondence for future reference and regulatory compliance.

## Building a Culture of Compliance

Compliance should not be viewed as an administrative burden. Organizations that integrate compliance into their governance systems are better able to attract donor support, strengthen public confidence, reduce legal risks, improve decision-making, demonstrate accountability and sustain long-term public benefit work.

Ultimately, compliance is part of good organizational leadership.

## Key Takeaways

- The Regulations provide the practical procedures that implement the PBO Act.
- Organizations should use the prescribed forms for all statutory applications and notifications.
- Annual reports must be submitted within six months after the end of the financial year.
- Material changes must be reported promptly using the prescribed procedures.
- It is essential to maintain accurate records and observe statutory timelines forcompliance.
- Effective compliance systems protect both the organization and the wider PBO sector.
- Compliance is an ongoing responsibility that extends throughout the lifecycle of every Public Benefit Organization.
